Good decisions start with good information.

  1. 1

    Decisions rest on information.

    Medicine is about making well-informed decisions.

  2. 2

    No one can read every page.

    It is not feasible for a healthcare provider to thoroughly review and understand every bit of clinical history about a patient. But AI can access all electronic documents and capture critical elements including major diagnoses, procedures, and drug or other sensitivities.

  3. 3

    Fluent text is not calibrated confidence.

    Current LLMs are very good at generating text and are getting better at not hallucinating. However, their confidence is still not well calibrated, and a long paragraph for each diagnosis is overload.

  4. 4

    Decision models answer specific questions.

    Decision models can provide well-calibrated answers to specific questions, making history summarization and detection of specific patient properties feasible and much less expensive than LLMs.

  5. 5

    Subscribe to the most relevant content for YOUR practice. Interactive adjudication of content.

    Medical summaries must be tailored to your practice—it’s not one-size-fits-all. Further, it is critical not to propagate incorrect information—sometimes a condition is misdiagnosed, and including that in the summary is dangerous.

  6. 6

    A deterministic workflow, using both.

    A deterministic workflow that integrates decision models and LLMs is likely to improve the efficiency and job satisfaction of healthcare providers the most.

In a zero-shot radiology evaluation on a single workstation, a 3-billion-parameter decision model came within 0.04 AUROC of a 27B one on report findings while using 3 to 9 times less energy per request. Reading a generative model out as a decision model cut calibration error on CT volumes from 0.436 to 0.072. No model won every task, so models and thresholds should be chosen on local cases.
